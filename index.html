A cosmic boarding pass, generated from a date

Born Among Stars

“We are a way for the cosmos to know itself.” — Carl Sagan

Give a day that matters. We turn it into an astronomer’s day-number, run the real orbital mechanics, find the star whose light from that day is arriving now, and assign you a destination somewhere in the universe — with a reason, and a transmission for the year ahead.

No account, no tracking — and no name. You don’t give us one; the cosmos issues you a callsign and an address. Every calculation happens in your browser. There is no server to send anything to — open DevTools and watch nothing leave.

The link reproduces this exact card — it’s deterministic. Share it, or send someone theirs.

Every number on a card is real and checkable — orbital periods from NASA fact sheets, star distances from Hipparcos and Gaia, the day-number from the standard Julian Day formula. Here is exactly how it works.

The destination is assigned by a SHA-256 hash of your date — fair and reproducible, not fate. The meaning is in the astronomy. Open source, MIT. Source on GitHub.
